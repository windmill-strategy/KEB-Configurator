## Overload Characteristic

The rated switching frequency determines the drive's overload capability and output current waveform quality. Higher switching frequencies produce smoother motor currents but reduce the available overload capacity.

### Available Ratings

**2 kHz** — Lowest switching frequency. Provides the highest overload current capacity. Best suited for heavy-duty applications with frequent load transients such as crushers, shredders, and heavy presses.

**4 kHz** — Standard switching frequency for most industrial applications. Offers a good balance between current waveform quality and overload capability.

**6 kHz** — Higher switching frequency for applications requiring improved motor current quality and reduced audible motor noise.

**8 kHz** — Highest switching frequency available. Produces the smoothest output waveform and lowest motor noise. Ideal for applications where acoustic performance is critical, but overload capacity is reduced.

### Trade-off

Higher switching frequencies improve motor efficiency and reduce acoustic noise, but increase drive losses and reduce the maximum overload current. Select the lowest frequency that meets your application's noise and performance requirements.
