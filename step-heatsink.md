## Heatsink & Mounting Configuration

The heatsink configuration determines how the drive dissipates heat and how it is physically installed in your enclosure or cabinet.

### Cooling Methods

**Air** — Standard forced-air cooling using an integrated fan. Suitable for most cabinet installations with adequate ventilation.

**Air (Convection)** — Passive convection cooling without a fan. Ideal for environments where fan noise or maintenance is a concern, but requires adequate airflow around the unit.

**Water** — Liquid-cooled heatsink for high power density installations. Significantly reduces cabinet thermal load and enables compact drive arrangements.

**Oil** — Oil-cooled configuration for specialized industrial environments. Provides excellent thermal performance in harsh conditions.

### Mounting Types

**Panel** — Standard panel mounting inside an electrical cabinet. The drive is fully enclosed within the cabinet and contributes its heat losses to the cabinet interior.

**Push-Through** — The heatsink protrudes through the back of the cabinet, dissipating heat outside the enclosure. This dramatically reduces the cooling requirements inside the cabinet.

**Book** — Compact side-by-side mounting format that minimizes cabinet width requirements.

**High Performance variants** — Panel or push-through mounting with enhanced heatsink surface area for improved thermal performance at higher switching frequencies or elevated ambient temperatures.
